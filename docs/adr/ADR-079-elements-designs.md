# ADR-079 — Offer complete editable designs in Elements

Status: Implemented in the 2026-10-09 working tree; **not committed** at the time of this record. This extends the Elements browser in [ADR-075](ADR-075-elements-and-presets.md).

## Context

The committed Elements browser inserts individual objects. A user may also want a complete slide composition as a starting point without choosing a deck template or asking the agent to construct the slide.

## Decision in the working tree

- A new Designs collection contains ten named compositions: possibility, momentum, wedding, webinar, conference, workshop, product, sale, testimonial, and thumbnail. `designs.ts` creates ordinary editable text, shapes, SVG artwork, and images; there is no new slide or element type. The visual assets are original, bundled WebP files. They are imported into the document when a slide is chosen, so the saved deck works offline.
- The collection renders each design with `ScaledSlide` using a standalone preview deck in the current deck language. Choosing a design creates a fresh slide after the current one, selects it, and batches its `asset.add` and `slide.add` commands as one undo step.
- The Shapes collection gains richer SVG previews, group-specific colors, responsive group layout, and direction-aware line/card art. These previews do not change the underlying insertion commands or saved object schema.
- The new labels and hint text have Hebrew and English entries. The design copy itself is authored in both languages. A preview is presentation data, while the inserted slide becomes ordinary deck content.

## Addition: fourteen more designs (2026-10-09)

The collection grows from eleven to twenty-five. The fourteen are for purposes the first eleven do not serve: a birthday invitation (`birthday`), a chef's dinner (`dinner`), a new team member (`teammate`), a live concert (`concert`), a Rosh Hashanah greeting (`roshhashana`), open positions (`hiring`), a book launch (`book`), a podcast episode (`podcast`), the opening of a neighbourhood shop (`opening`), an open house (`openhouse`), enrolment to a course (`course`), a milestone in one number (`milestone`), a sunrise yoga class (`yoga`) and a community volunteer day (`volunteer`). In the gallery they follow the eleven in an order that alternates light and dark.

- **One layout was not to be repeated fourteen times.** Nine of the first eleven are a full-bleed picture, a wash and the copy on one side. Each of the fourteen starts from a layout of its own: copy in the empty middle of a picture whose subject lies around its edges (`birthday`, `milestone`), a plate in a round window beside a numbered menu (`dinner`), a portrait in a rounded frame with chips of facts (`teammate`), a poster with a line set on its side (`concert`), an arched window beside centred serif type (`roshhashana`), a hard split with a badge on the seam (`hiring`), a title set on the blank cover of the book in the picture (`book`), square cover art over a turned card beside a very large episode number (`podcast`), a turned postcard under a drawn awning (`opening`), a listing sheet of picture, sidebar of figures and price strip (`openhouse`), a cut-out student in front of drawn blocks of colour (`course`), light type on an open sky (`yoga`), and a picture in a soft blob (`volunteer`). Five are on a light ground, eight on a dark or saturated one, and `openhouse` is both. Each is set in its own choice of the built-in families (book serifs, a slab, a condensed poster face, a rounded one, light and heavy sans faces), so that the type differs as the layouts do.
- **Pictures are generated, as the user asked.** `docs/reference-decks/scripts/picture.mjs` (Codex CLI, ADR-004) gained three options so that it serves this collection too, in place of a second copy of it: `--out` (where the WebP goes; the templates' asset records are then left alone), `--work` (where the PNG and the ledger of calls are kept) and `--max-kb`. The budget was 24 calls; fourteen were used and none was taken again. Each picture was asked for in the shape its slide shows it in, and the slide was composed around what came. The prompts are in `design-assets/PROMPTS.md`.
- **What the helpers of `designs.ts` gained.** A flat ground is the slide's own `background` (`grounds`), not a rectangle of the slide's size that a drag could move. `label` is a shape that carries its own words (a button, a chip, a sticker): one element to move and to edit. `photo` takes a mask, a crop, a rotation and a shadow; `shape` takes a preset and a corner as a share of the shorter side (the `radius` of the first designs is ignored by a `roundRect`); `words` takes a line height.
- **The layer rule holds, and a test now says so.** Everything that frames a picture lies under it; over a picture there are words only, and in `milestone` a soft shade in the middle, away from the confetti that is its subject. `designs.test.ts` keeps a point on the subject of each of the fourteen pictures and fails when anything but the picture is the top element there, in either direction. In `book` the title lies on the cover on purpose, so its point is on the cover below the words.
- **What was checked.** Every design was rendered in Hebrew and in English at 1920 and at 264 wide and looked at; every line was measured against its box, and a line set in the deck's own font fills at most 85% of its box, since another template's font may be wider. The app's design check (every rule) reports one finding for each of the fourteen in both languages: L11, fonts and colours that are not the template's. In the app, once per design in each language: the tile shows, a click adds the slide and its picture in one undo step, a click on the picture's subject selects the picture, and one undo removes both. The gallery draws all twenty-five previews live: it opened in 0.4 s and scrolled at 60 frames a second, with two or three long frames (50 to 85 ms) the first time the pictures come into view; nothing was changed for it.
- **A test that was failing.** `shell/designRules.test.ts` forbids colour literals in the app's components, and had failed on `designs.ts` since the first ten designs were committed. The file now stands in that test's list of exemptions beside the palettes of `templates/curated.ts`: its colours are what the slides are drawn in, not what the app is drawn in.
- **Not done.** The eleven earlier designs were left as they were. The design check that the fourteen pass still has findings on nine of them (text of low contrast in `momentum`, `wedding`, `workshop`, `testimonial` and `thumbnail`; a line that runs out of its box in English in `wedding` and `workshop`; edges that nearly line up in most), which is work of its own. One test of `e2e/elements-panel.spec.ts`, on paging through the icon collection, fails on the committed code as well and has nothing to do with the designs. The packaged build was not made or run.

## Addition: a review of all twenty-five (2026-10-09)

The user asked for a pass over every design: that the typefaces fit and that no slide is "too empty" (the screenshot was the Hebrew `roshhashana`), and, during the work, that where possible the background be separate from the central picture, which should be transparent, so that the picture stays usable when the background is changed.

- **A design carries its whole type.** Until now a design set its headline in a face of its own and left every other line to the deck's fonts: its eyebrow, body and details changed with the template, and the first ten leaned on Georgia and Impact, which the app does not bundle. Now every run names its face. Each case sets the faces it means (a display face and a text face, chosen per script), and `faces` in `designs.ts` gives each design one Hebrew and one Latin face for any line that sets none (`setIn`); `designs.test.ts` fails on a run without a face. All of them are faces the app bundles. `thumbnail` no longer needs Impact: it is set in heavy italic Rubik and Poppins.
- **Subjects are cut-outs, and the background is the slide's.** Thirteen designs show a subject of their own: the speaker (`webinar`), the client (`testimonial`), the lamp (`product`), the table of things (`sale`), the new colleague (`teammate`), the fruit (`roshhashana`), the chair (`hiring`), the microphone (`podcast`), the singer (`concert`), the book (`book`), the plate (`dinner`), and the two that already were cut-outs (`thumbnail`, `course`). In each, the picture is the subject alone on a transparent ground, in a frame of its own size. Behind it are the slide's `background` (`grounds`) and drawn shapes: a disc, an arch, a ring, a pool of light, a cast shadow. Eleven of the thirteen were recomposed for this; four of them (`webinar`, `testimonial`, `product`, `sale`) had been a full-bleed photograph under a wash. The other twelve designs are a photograph that is itself the ground (a landscape, a table seen from above, a building, a sky) or a framed photograph on a flat ground (`opening`, `openhouse`, `volunteer`): there is no subject to lift out without losing the picture.
- **Two pictures in one design.** `concert` and `book` keep a photograph behind their subject. `designSlide` and `insertDesign` take a second asset for it, `designAssets.ts` has `previewBackdrop` and `designBackdropFile`, and the gallery imports both files and adds both assets and the slide in one undo step.
- **Fuller slides.** What looked empty got content of the kind its purpose has, not ornament. `roshhashana`: the fruit is larger than its window and stands in front of it, under a drawn moon and stars; the Hebrew lines are a step larger and all in Frank Ruhl Libre; the emblem has a laurel. `webinar`: a row of when, time and where, the speaker's name card, a call to action. `product`: three figures, a price, a call to action. `sale`: the offer as one lock-up, the dates, four categories, a coupon code. `testimonial`: a larger serif quote with a drawn quotation mark, the name and role on two lines, the rating. `wedding`: the card recomposed around a date row between two rules, the reception and the ceremony, a reply-by line. `conference`: three figures and the venue. `workshop`: three crafts and a line of place, seats and price. `momentum`: its three words again as numbered steps. `yoga`: a row of details over a pale haze on the water, on the copy's side only. `podcast`, `concert`, `dinner`, `possibility`, `volunteer`: a line or an ornament each. Stale years in the first ten (2026) were moved to 2027.
- **Helpers.** `pool` (a soft radial pool of colour: a glow, or the shadow under a cut-out), `arch` (a window with a round head) and `sparkle` join the helpers of `designs.ts`. `picture.mjs` gains `--trim` (cut to the box of what is not transparent) and `--circle` (keep a circle of the source, for the plate). Twelve pictures were generated, none taken again; the prompts are in `design-assets/PROMPTS.md`. The bundled pictures weigh 4.8 MB (27 files), up from 3.9 MB.
- **What was checked.** Every design was rendered in Hebrew and in English at 1920 and at 264 wide and looked at, round after round; every line was measured against its box. The app's design check (every rule) now reports L11 only (fonts and colours that are not the template's) for all twenty-five in both languages; before, nine of the first eleven had other findings. In the app, for each design in each language: the tile shows, a click adds the slide and its one or two pictures in one undo step, a click on the picture's subject selects the picture, the Design check panel shows L11 only, and one undo removes everything. `designs.test.ts` holds a point on the subject of all twenty-five.
- **Not done.** The packaged build was not made or run, and of the E2E suite only the designs test of `e2e/elements-panel.spec.ts` ran. `shell/designRules.test.ts` fails on `frames.ts` and `FramesCollection.tsx`, which belong to the Frames collection, not to this work.

## Addition: a hundred more designs, in ten groups (2026-10-10)

The user asked for a hundred more ready-made slides: the collection grows from twenty-five to 125.

- **Ten groups by what a design is for, ten new designs in each.** Presentation slides (`deck`: title, agenda, section divider, about us, process, case study, roadmap, closing, Q&A, team), business and team (`business`), marketing and sales (`marketing`), social media (`social`), celebrations and invitations (`celebrations`), holidays and greetings (`holidays`), culture and going out (`culture`), learning and kids (`learning`), community (`community`) and local business (`local`). The first twenty-five are filed under the same groups (`baseGroups` in `designs.ts`). Presentation slides are a new kind: the slides every deck needs, each as a finished design.
- **All hundred were planned before any was drawn, so that they differ.** For each design the plan fixed what it says, its structure, the kind and format of its picture, a light or dark ground and its faces. Among the structures: words set on an object in the picture (a cinema screen, a sticky note, a letter, the screen of an auditorium); very large type behind a cut-out that stands in front of it; a subject that hangs from the top edge or rises from the bottom one; photographs split in two (before and after, this or that); a picture in a window of a shape (a drop, a half circle, a slanted window, an oval, a postage stamp, a strip of film, a phone); content that is itself the composition (a menu, a price list, a timetable, a timeline, numbered steps, tiles, a punch card, tear-off tabs, pricing cards, a dashboard); and photographs that are the ground, with the copy in their calm area. Ten agents built the ten groups at the same time, each from the plan and one brief. The lead looked at every design's render in Hebrew and in English, as sheets of ten at 460 pixels a slide and at full size for a sample of thirteen, before a group was accepted; the agent of each group looked at every one of its renders at full size.
- **Where the code is.** The helpers of `designs.ts` moved to `designKit.ts`, with the `Design` record (its faces, its ground, a point on its picture's subject, and `compose`) and `drawDesign`. Each group's ten are a file, `design-packs/<group>.ts`, an object of `Design` records by id. `designs.ts` keeps the first twenty-five as they were and wires the files in: `DESIGN_PACKS`, `DESIGN_GROUPS`, `designsOf(group)`, and `DESIGN_IDS` in the order the gallery shows them. `designAssets.ts` no longer imports each picture by name: it takes every `.webp` of `design-assets/` by its file name, and a backdrop is whatever `<id>-bg.webp` is there.
- **A picture's size is known before a deck imports it.** A design is drawn in the gallery from its bundled file, and the renderer applies a `crop` only to a picture whose record has a width and a height: without them the four portraits of `team` each showed the whole sheet. `design-assets/sizes.json` holds every picture's size, `scripts/design-asset-sizes.mjs` writes it, and `designs.test.ts` fails when a picture was added or replaced without it.
- **The gallery shows groups.** With 125 designs one list of live slides is no longer usable: the collection opens on the ten groups with the first two designs of each, "Show all" opens one group, and a search field finds designs by name in every group. A tile draws its slide only once it has come near the visible part of the panel (`useNear`, now exported from `StickerGrid.tsx`). Measured in a browser while another job was rendering on the same machine: the first slide is drawn 0.24 s after the click, three slides are drawn before any scroll, and a first fast scroll through the twenty tiles had eleven frames over 50 ms (the longest 106 ms) while the pictures decoded; a second scroll had none over 19 ms.
- **The pictures leave the executable.** The folder weighs 20 MB (127 files; it was 4.8 MB). `build/media.ts` has a rule for it, so a production build writes the pictures to `media/images/designs/` beside the executable and the bundle keeps only their addresses, as it does for the templates' photographs ([ADR-074](ADR-074-packaged-media-library.md)).
- **Pictures are generated.** One call of Codex CLI for each of the hundred and a second take of three: 103 calls of a budget of 150. 51 are cut-outs on a transparent ground; none of the hundred needed a second picture as a backdrop. The prompts, and how the calls were kept to the budget, are in `design-assets/PROMPTS.md`.
- **What the review taught.** Text with `autoFit: 'shrink'` whose lines are taller than its box is drawn smaller without any sign of it, and a large display face has a line box about one and a half times its size: the review tool now reports it, and every size in the ten files is the size that is drawn. The design check counts words (L13: sixty a slide), which sets how much a menu or a pricing table can say. The Design check popover of the app lists what is to be acted on; fonts and colours that are not the template's (L11) are information and are not listed, so a clean design lists nothing. A dev server without hot reload freezes the lists of `import.meta.glob`, so the review page asks for a pack and a picture by path.
- **Tests.** `designs.test.ts` counts 125 and checks every one of them in both languages: a valid slide, one picture file (two with a backdrop; a design may show its one file in several frames), words in at least three elements, a face on every run, and the picture on top at the point on its subject, which a design of a pack names itself. It also checks the groups and the sizes table. `shell/designRules.test.ts` exempts `designKit.ts` and the folder `design-packs/`: their colours are what the slides are drawn in. The Elements E2E spec covers the groups, "Show all", the search and the two insertions.
- **What was checked.** Typecheck, ESLint and Prettier on what was touched; the Elements unit tests (73) and the whole Elements E2E spec (16) pass. Every one of the hundred was rendered in Hebrew and in English at 1920 and at 264 wide, and the app's design check (every rule, with the pictures' real sizes) reports L11 only for all of them in both languages. In the app, once for each of the hundred, the languages taking turns: the tile is found by a search and shows its slide, a click adds the slide and its picture in one undo step, a click on the point on the picture's subject selects the picture, the Design check lists nothing for the slide, and one undo removes everything; a control run with a line of 10px text is reported by the same reading. A build of one entry through the media plugin into a scratch folder wrote the 127 pictures to `images/designs/` and left none in the bundle.
- **Not done.** The packaged app was not built or run, so the pictures were not loaded from the media library by the real executable. The rest of the E2E suite did not run. Each design was clicked in the app in one language, not in both. Invented names, phones and addresses were not checked against real ones; the car of `carwash` resembles a well-known classic model, without a badge or a plate. The first twenty-five were not touched: the stricter review tool finds texts among them that are drawn smaller than their nominal size or whose line box leaves its frame (the look is the one that was reviewed), and the app's design check still reports L11 only for them.
