# ADR-109 — Ready slides are read in one language, and shown as pictures made ahead

Status: Implemented in the 2026-10-21 working tree, with Slidr Library's designs section (slidrhq/library); **not committed**, and **not published**: until the library is published with the files below, the app and the library read the whole catalogs as before. It changes how the designs of [ADR-079](ADR-079-elements-designs.md) and [ADR-106](ADR-106-ready-slides-panel.md) are read from the media library of [ADR-090](ADR-090-media-library-on-the-web.md).

## Request

"The loading time of the elements in the library (for example 'Ready designs') is very long! It has become much worse." Then, on seeing the cause: "Should we consider SQLite? Something more efficient?" (no, below), "I also saw that the designs have no preview, and are built again every time. Is it so in the app too? Is it efficient?", and "Yes. Note that the size in the app and in the library differ a little; if it helps, I don't mind it being kept twice."

## Context

- `elements/designs/index.json` had grown from about 5 MB to 40.6 MB in a few days: 286 designs, each now in sixteen languages, about 2.5 MB a language. `managed.json` was 7 MB. Above the 25 MiB of Pages, the catalog was served in parts by a Function.
- A gallery shows one language, and every reader read all sixteen. The app read both catalogs as it started (top-level `await` in `designs.ts` and `managedDesigns.ts`, which every page of the shell imports through `register.tsx`), and kept 4,576 slides in memory for the session.
- The core fetched the library without compression: reqwest was built without `gzip` and `brotli`, so after each publication the app downloaded 42 MB for the catalog that Pages sends as 2.2 MB, and 16 MB for the templates' 2 MB.
- Each tile of the gallery drew its whole slide live (`ScaledSlide`): 131 elements, the fonts of its text, and the design's picture at full size, 153 KB and 1,132 pixels wide on average, for a tile 160 pixels wide. The first screens of the app's gallery read 12.9 MB of pictures and decoded 108 megapixels. Slidr Library, which lists every design, read their pictures as it scrolled: 471 megapixels for the Hebrew designs. The frames had had the same trouble, and had been given pictures made ahead (`frameThumbs.ts`).
- Measured before the change, on this machine: Slidr Library's designs section 1.06–1.38 s and 75–122 MB of heap (its templates and card sets, 1.0 and 0.33 s); the app's heap after it started, 202–232 MB.

## Decision

- **Each catalog is published in each language as well.** Publishing (`libraryFiles` in `build/media.ts`, `designFiles`) writes `elements/designs/<language>/index.json` and `managed.json`: the catalog with the slides of that language alone (2.56 MB for Hebrew, 242 KB in brotli), the languages every design is drawn in (`languages`), and the hash of the file it was made from (`from`). The whole catalogs stay as they are, for the apps already installed and for the authors' scripts.
- **A file made from another catalog is not read.** A copy pulled from the site (`media:pull`) holds these files as they were, and a publisher that does not write them (the studio's copy of the publishing script, until it is taken again) would publish them beside a newer catalog. So a reader takes such a file only when its `from` names the hashes the manifest has (`madeFrom`), and otherwise reads the whole catalog and keeps the language it needs. Publishing writes them again every time, in place of those of a pulled copy.
- **The app reads the designs of its language when the gallery is first drawn**, not when it starts: `loadDesigns(lang)` (and `loadAllDesigns()`, every language, for the tests and the review page). The gallery asks for them with `use()` under a `Suspense`, not in an effect: the panel is drawn hidden at the shell's quiet moment, and a hidden `Activity` runs no effects, so they are read then and are there when the panel is opened. Before they come, the panel shows the tiles of a group empty.
- **Each design has a picture of itself in each language, at two widths**: `images/designs/thumbs/<width>/<language>/<design>.webp`, 320 pixels wide (a tile of the app's panel, 9 KB on average) and 640 (a card of Slidr Library, 23 KB), with `index.json`. The galleries give both to the browser (`srcset`), which takes what the tile needs at the screen's density. `scripts/generate-design-thumbs.mjs` makes them of the renderer's own drawing (`dev/design-thumbs.html`), from the library as publishing stages it, a dev server of its own serving it.
- **A picture is shown only while its design is as it was drawn.** Publishing writes `elements/designs/revs.json`: a word for each design in each language, a hash of its slide, the hashes of its pictures and `DESIGN_DRAWING`, with what it was made from. The script writes the word each picture was drawn from into its index. A design whose words differ, or that has no picture, is drawn live as before. A gallery compares two strings, and reads no slide to know: Slidr Library shows a design in all its languages without reading them. A change to how the renderer draws, or to a font, changes no word: `DESIGN_DRAWING` is raised and the script run with `--all`.
- **The core asks for compressed answers** (`gzip`, `brotli` in reqwest). What it keeps under a hash is the file itself, as before.
- **Not SQLite.** The library is read-only content published in one go and read whole a view at a time, by four readers (the app's core and its pages, Slidr Library in a browser, Node for the tests and scripts, the studio in Python), each of which reads a static file today. A database file would be one file that changes whole at every publication, kept by the core under one hash; it would be served uncompressed (JSON goes out at a twentieth of its size); the browser would need SQLite in WebAssembly and range requests; and the limit of 25 MiB a file stays. What was slow was reading sixteen languages for one, and drawing what a picture shows.

## Consequences

- Measured with the library as the next publication will have it (the author copy staged as publishing stages it, with the pictures, served by Playwright in place of the site), against the published library, Hebrew, on this machine:
  - The app's gallery, the 84 tiles of its first screen scrolled through: 0.27 s instead of 1.6 s (20–38 ms a screen, 95–244 before); 1.2 megapixels decoded instead of 108; 0.25 s of long tasks instead of 1.3; 145 MB of heap instead of 168–174. It reads `he/index.json`, `he/managed.json` and `revs.json`, and no picture of a design at full size.
  - Slidr Library's designs, all of them scrolled through: 1.05–1.18 s instead of 8.6–11.0 s; 12 megapixels instead of 471; 0.05 s of long tasks instead of 6.8–7.5; 43–44 MB of heap instead of 94–124. Its first card, in its dev server, 0.9–1.1 s instead of 1.1–1.5 s.
  - The app no longer reads the designs as it starts: on the published library it was up in 1.06–1.13 s in these runs, where it had been 1.40–1.51 s.
- The library grows by 10,752 pictures, 186 MB, to about 14,000 files: Pages takes 20,000 on its free plan. A viewer reads only the pictures of what it shows. A third width, or many more designs, would need the file count looked at again.
- The pictures are as current as the last run of the script. After a design or its picture changes, it is drawn live in the galleries until the script runs and the library is published.
- `managedDesigns.ts` is gone: the managed catalog is read with the authored one, in a language. Slidr Library reads `managed.json` itself for its upload form, and asks the English designs for the sizes of a published design's pictures.

## What was checked

- Unit tests: `build/media.test.ts` (each catalog in each of its languages, with what it was made from; a word that changes with a design's slide or picture and with nothing else; the files of a pulled copy made again on publishing), `designThumbs.test.ts` (on a library the publisher's own `designFiles` wrote: a language read from its file and not the whole catalog; none read in a language the publication drew none in; the whole catalog read for a library published without the files, or with files of another catalog; a picture shown at both widths while its design is as drawn, and not for a design changed since or words not of the library published), `designs.test.ts` (every language read on its own lists the designs, languages, text and picture sizes the whole catalogs give it, for all sixteen, against the published library). The whole suite: 3,139 pass; five fail before and after this change, on the published library's content (286 authored designs of 296, one design in `invitations`, `sizes.json` sought at a path that is not there since the desktop app moved, 31 templates of 32) and on an inline style in `collections.tsx`.
- Rust: `a_file_comes_compressed_and_is_kept_as_itself` (an answer in gzip is kept as the file, and the request asks for br and gzip); the sixteen tests of `media_library` pass; Clippy and rustfmt.
- `elements-panel.spec.ts`: a group and back, a search, a design with one picture and one with two added in one undo step each, and the German designs, each in German (the pictures carry their `lang`). "designs are shown by group" wants 19 groups where the published `managed.json` makes 21, before and after this change.
- The pictures of all sixteen languages (5,376 designs, two widths) were made in a scratch folder, not in the author copy, and looked at in Hebrew, Arabic, Hindi and Japanese: the type of each language, right to left where it should be, the photographs in place. The app's panel was rendered with them, every tile a picture.
- Typecheck, ESLint and Prettier of the app and of Slidr Library.
