# ADR-094 — The objects of a layout are the slide's

Status: Implemented in the 2026-10-14 working tree, first for the Zerem template as a trial and then, at the user's request, for all twenty-one; **not committed** at the time of this record. This extends the template engine in [ADR-023](ADR-023-template-engine.md) and the built-in catalog in [ADR-076](ADR-076-template-layouts.md).

## Context

The user found the templates too rigid: only the text of a slide could be changed, and almost everything else a template draws (cards, numbered tiles, bars, the drawing beside the text) was part of the layout, drawn under the slide and out of reach. They also asked for more room on the text slide, with the drawing on its far side much less prominent. They asked for the change on Zerem alone, to judge it, and then for the rest of the library.

## Decision in the working tree

- **The model.** `Layout.objects` is an optional list of elements that a new slide of the layout starts with, under the elements of its placeholders. The slide holds copies, so they are selected, moved, recoloured and deleted like anything put there by hand. `decorations` stays for what frames every slide and is not the slide's to change: the foot (its rule, the logo, the slide's number) and the deck's footer. `layout.update` takes `objects` too. A deck without them is unchanged, and no migration is needed.
- **Names.** `slideFromLayout` puts the copies first (`layoutObjects`), each named after the slide and its object (`<slide>_<object>`, a new id only when that name is taken). A copy of the slide names them after the copy (`copySlide`).
- **What is the layout's.** A copy is known by its name, whatever the user did to it (`layoutCopies`): moved, resized, recoloured or animated, it is still the template's drawing. One under another name, made before copies were named, is known by drawing exactly as its object. Two things are the user's: a duplicate of an object, which has a new name, and a copy the user wrote words in, since a switch never takes typed text away.
- **Changing layout or template.** The layout's copies go with it and the new layout's come in at the bottom of the slide, in its order (`changeLayout`, `objectsFollow` in `applyTemplate`). A slide that had no layout, drawn by hand or written as HTML, takes none when it takes one: it was designed as a whole, and only the layout's decorations show under it. A test deck's opening slide on a ground of the primary colour showed why: Sirtut's drawing, in that colour, came in over it. What is the user's stays, as any element without a seat does. Two layouts with the same objects change nothing, so what the user deleted stays deleted. Because the names come back, a deck switched to another template and back is still the deck it was (ADR-023). The theme's corner radius and shadow are handed only to what stays.
- **Turning the direction.** The deck mirrors the copies as it mirrors everything, changed ones too. Where the template drew the layout by hand for the other direction, a copy still exactly as its object, and not animated, becomes what was drawn (`untouchedObjects`).
- **Elsewhere.** The New slide picker draws each layout with its objects. A standard text layout derived from a content layout takes the objects around its title and foot, as it takes decorations.
- **Zerem.** Its cards, tiles and step numbers, the timeline's axis and dots, the comparison's panels, the process arrows, the quote mark, the section's field, the closing's checks and the stream are objects. The stream is one group, framed tight around its lanes so a press beside them does not pick it. The text layout's panel now runs from the start margin to 244px from the far edge (it was 1128px wide, now 1580px), with a body of 1448px. The stream is a dim strip at 45% strength that runs in under the panel. The text sample is longer, to show the room.

## Consequences and checks

The other twenty built-in templates still draw everything as decorations. The agent's layout listing does not mention objects, and a template the agent drafts from HTML (`draft.ts`) makes decorations only. At first a copy the user had changed stayed on a slide that moved to another template. The user resized the field of Zerem's section slide, switched to Layla, and found it still over Layla's drawing, so a changed copy now goes with its template too. Replayed in the editor (the handle pulled, then Layla applied), the field went.

`packages/templates/src/objects.test.ts` covers the copies on a new slide, content by role beside them, change of layout, what the user changed or animated, a switch of template and back, a turn of direction and back, and a layout drawn by hand for the other direction. The existing tests of the model and the template engine pass, and so does the round trip of every pair of built-in templates (`builtin.test.ts`). Zerem passed its acceptance run in the browser (`acceptDraft`): every layout in both languages with no error and no L07 or L16, the background variant, the model's decks, and its sample deck moved to and from each of the other twenty templates. A first run failed once: the longer English text slide overflowed Tzuk's text box by 37px, and the text was shortened. In the editor (Playwright against the dev server), a press on the empty foot of the text panel selected the panel and the arrow keys moved it. It stayed through a change of layout and a switch to Tzuk, while the untouched objects were replaced. On the same day the full unit run had failures this change does not touch: the size of the agent's element schema, the runtime bundle hash, the design rules of components, and the font pair Gan and Bina share.

## Addition: the whole library (2026-10-14)

- **The other twenty templates** give their slides objects through `withObjects` (`builtin/kit.ts`), called at the end of each template's function before its sample is drawn. Zerem names its objects by hand. Every decoration goes over except what frames the slide (`framesTheSlide`): the master components, what starts in the foot band (24px above the highest of them in the lower half), a hidden one, and a ground that covers 90% of the slide. Anything a decoration that stays is drawn over also stays, repeatedly, because objects draw over every decoration: the card under a slide number would otherwise cover it. Groups are fitted to their children (ARR-01) as they go over. Bina redraws Gan's layouts, and its `illustrated` now redraws Gan's objects too.
- **Checked by pixels.** Every sample deck of the twenty, in Hebrew and English, was drawn before and after the split and compared. The first split hid the slide number or the logo in Shvil, Layla, Nof, Bolet and Kod, which led to the rule above. After it the decks matched, apart from one pixel of anti-aliasing and one spot on Ziv that a full-size render showed to be render noise.
- **The text slide of every template** was redrawn twice, five agents at once, each on four templates, against the Zerem result. In the first round the body went to 1400–1500px with the drawing reduced to a strip at the far side. The user found the box still too small and the gap under it too large, and asked for the text to start at the top. In the second round, Zerem's included, the card or panel runs from margin to margin (about 1728px), down to 24–32px above the foot. The body inside it is about 1600px wide and starts at the top (`vAlign` top, no longer `middle`). The drawing keeps only a small accent in the margin or on a corner of the card. In the first round the accents were: Shvil's trail and sun, Tzuk's cliff, Lavan's scale plate, Layla's slanted bars, Zohar's orbit, Migdal's slim tower, Gan's rainbow, Nof's ridge, Defus's print strip, Ariach's tile column, Bolet's burst, Hod's doors behind the plaque, Sirtut's detail callout, Ziv's ring, Shidur's signal badge, Bina's small network, Mifgash's call tiles, Kod's minimap, Luach's picked note and Magen's honeycomb strip. Each text sample got a two-line lead and four one-line points. The decks were drawn again afterwards: only the text slide differs from the split.
- **Left as it is.** Bina's robot, made smaller, still holds its sign at the corner beside the title. The longer samples are past the 60-word note of the design check, as Zerem's is. A text slide whose text is short now leaves the lower part of its box empty, as asked. On a template without a card, a slide moved into it can get the design check's warning about an empty band at the foot of the content.
- **What the design check now sees.** Text the layouts drew (step numbers, figure names, measures) is now the slide's, so the design check judges it. On Sirtut's team slide the `FIG. n` names stood on the white dimension line that ran through their letters, and failed the contrast rule. The line now breaks under each name, as a dimension's figure breaks its line (`chainGaps`).
- **The acceptance tests.** A deck with a cover photo on its opening slide (Lavan, Nof, Defus), moved to a template that has no seat for one, keeps the photo where it was. The photo lies over the new layout's drawing, and the check reports the contrast of a word of the drawing under it (Sirtut's measure). `knownSwitchError` already passed the same case for the photos of Shvil's cards. It now also passes a contrast finding on a slide that came from such an opening layout, when every element the finding names is a copy of the new layout's objects.

## Addition: the technology templates and Atlas (2026-10-17)

- **Request.** The user found that the latest templates, Anan, Shvav, Mutzar and Atlas, did not follow this record: on their slides only the text could be changed. They asked for the rule to be set in the studio too.
- **Cause.** The four are authored in `packages/templates/src/authoring`, after this record, and never went through `withObjects`: every drawing was a decoration (no objects at all, where the other templates of the library have 59 to 206).
- **The fix.** Each now calls `withObjects` before its sample is drawn. Anan, Mutzar and Atlas split as they were drawn. Shvav drew ten scenes each as one drawing the size of the slide, which `framesTheSlide` keeps as a ground: the opening board, the section's chip, the big number's package, the quote's block, the text slide's bus, the edge of the full picture, the castellations of the cards, the timeline's trace, the signal chain and the two boards of the comparison. They are now cut into their parts (`parts` in `shvav.ts`), each an SVG whose viewBox is a window on the slide's own coordinates, so the parts meet as they were drawn. The picture's courtyard on the text-and-image slide reached 4 px into the foot, and now stops short of it. Two drawings run on into the foot by design, the test points' trace on the big number slide and the reel's tape on the table slide: they come after the foot in the layout, which is then not drawn over them, so they are the slide's whole. Cut at the foot, they showed a seam at every size but the full one. Anan's dotted connector stood 4 px above its foot and went to the slide with the drawings; it is the foot's, and now stands in the foot's band. Objects: Anan 140, Shvav 89 (40 before the cut), Mutzar 223, Atlas 78. What stays is the foot, the grounds (Mutzar's dotted board, Atlas's neat line), and what the foot stands on (Anan's band under the full picture, Mutzar's glow on the quote).
- **The check.** `lockedDrawings` (`builtin/kit.ts`) names the drawings a layout keeps that its slides should hold: every one that does not frame the slide when no layout of the template gives objects, and otherwise one that stays only because a decoration drawn over it reaches into it without standing on it. `authoring/check.ts` fails a template on either, and lists the grounds and what the foot stands on, to look at.
- **The studio.** `AGENTS.md`, which the studio gives every conversation as its quality bar, now says it of templates. The studio's render of a template's sample measures each slide: the share of what it shows that is its own, the master components left out, shown beside the slide as "N% editable". A slide whose layout keeps drawings gets the finding `S-locked`; the slide it was written from has it too, so it is the template's and not counted against the language.
- **Checked.** Every slide of the four, in Hebrew and English, rendered before and after at 960 px (a first comparison read a dev server that had not seen the edits, and was taken again): Atlas identical; Anan's connector 4 px lower; elsewhere at most 36 levels of 255, the soft shadows of cards that are now objects, drawn over the foot as in every other template, and the rasterizing of a few edges. The design check now judges the drawings too: it finds near-alignments (L09) in them, about one a slide on Anan, more on Mutzar's interface mockups, as it does on Kod (21 in its sample) and Magen (14); warnings, not errors. Against a staged copy of the library with the four merged into it: their acceptance tests (34) passed, and in the editor, on every layout of each in both languages, a press on a drawing selected it, an arrow moved it and Delete removed it (120 of 120). A Hebrew deck turned to left to right took the left-to-right drawing for each of its 84 drawings with words, and turned back was the deck it was. The unit tests of the templates package (309) and of the studio's server (37) pass. On real data, the studio's measure gave the four 33 to 62% editable on average as the library has them, with every slide flagged, and 85 to 92% with none flagged after the fix.
- **Left.** Words drawn inside drawings are still letters of the SVG (Mutzar's command palette, comments and badges, Anan's region chips, Shvav's silkscreen): the drawing is the slide's, but its words cannot be edited as text. The rule also finds locks in older templates of the library: Shvil's closing hill, Ariach's timeline cards, Bolet's frame on the text-and-image slide and figure on the comparison, Ziv's big-number tile, Shidur's closing shade, Kod's section tabs and Luach's notes on the team slide. The library is not published with the fix yet.

## Addition: the text slide of the four (2026-10-17)

- **Request.** The user found that the size and place of the text on the text slide, set for the library in the second round above, had not been applied to the same four templates, and asked for it to be fixed and written into the instructions, the studio's too.
- **What they had.** A body 1000 to 1080 px wide beside a large drawing: Anan's migration scene, Shvav's radio module and its pinout, Mutzar's comment rail, Atlas's map inset, where the body was also centred in its box. Shvav, Mutzar and Atlas drew no foot on the slide. The sample was a line of lead and four bullets.
- **What they have now.** The title across the top, and under it a card in the template's own form from margin to margin, down to 24–32 px above the foot, the body in it from the top: Anan's white console card (1664 wide, its margins being 128), with a small region and its cloud standing on the card's corner beside the title; Shvav's module, castellated down both sides with traces off the board, the parts and the lit LED along its foot at the end corner; Mutzar's document window under its address bar, with a band of the page's cover and the people on the page beside the title, as on its other window slides; Atlas's sheet of paper with a stamp and its postmark on the corner. The body is 1520 to 1568 px wide. Each slide has the template's foot. The drawings it had are gone (`migrationScene`, `pinoutArt`, `commentRail`).
- **The sample.** A lead of two lines and four numbered points of one line, each opening in bold, in Hebrew and English, and in Atlas's sixteen languages. Atlas's copy types the agenda so (`agenda.items` pairs, `agenda.intro` a string or its lines); Japanese, Korean and Chinese give the lead in two lines broken at a phrase, since the renderer breaks them anywhere.
- **The rule.** `AGENTS.md` says it of every template; `textSlideGaps` in `authoring/check.ts` fails a text layout whose body is under 1500 px wide, not set from the top, or short of the foot, a title under 1300 px, and a sample that is not a lead and four points opening in bold. The other twenty-one templates of the library pass it in every language of their samples; the four did not, before. The studio's prompt for a template's sample (`template_prompts.py`) asks for the same shape in every language it writes, and lets a lead be given in its lines.
- **Checked.** The text slide of each, at full size and as a thumbnail, in each of its languages: no text out of its box, and the leads looked at line by line. Two Hebrew leads first ended in a tail of three words, and Russian's ran to a third line; they were rewritten. The other slides of the four were compared with their renders before the change: only the differences listed in the addition above.
