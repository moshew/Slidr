# ADR-087 — A hundred event magnets: labels as elements of their own, and the magnets by set

Status: Implemented in the 2026-10-10 working tree; **not committed** at the time of this record. This extends the event magnets of [ADR-083](ADR-083-event-magnets.md) and keeps the rule of [ADR-086](ADR-086-frames-keep-their-artwork.md): the artwork of a frame keeps its size, and the photograph takes the change.

## Context

The user asked for ninety more magnets, a hundred in all, and for designs that are more dynamic than the first ten: those were, nine times out of ten, a full card with about three stickers on three corners and one caption centred in a band below. Their example was the birthday magnet, whose ribbon was drawn into the card while the words only lay over it: "I would expect the background of 'Happy birthday…' to be an element too, and not part of the picture." Generated pictures were allowed, through Codex CLI as the ready-made designs use it. On the same night the user decided, in another session, that the content of Elements and the app's resources are kept in a media library beside the repository (`../Slidr-media`), not in the project tree.

## Decision in the working tree

- **Ground is card; an object is an element.** The card of a magnet keeps only what is truly ground: its fill, a pattern or texture, scattered small ornaments, the line around the window. Whatever reads as an object goes onto the slide as an element beside the picture, where it is moved, turned, typed over or deleted.
- **A third kind of extra, the label: a plate that carries words.** A ribbon, a badge, a tag, a chip, a speech bubble, a sign. In the catalogue it is `{ kind: 'label', art, x, y, w, h, rotation?, lines }`: the plate is a drawing of the catalogue's `stickers`, drawn in the units of the card, and the boxes of its lines are written from the plate's own corner. On a slide it is a group of its own inside the magnet's group (`frame:<id>:label:<art>`): the plate (`svg`) and its lines (`text`, each named `frame:<id>:caption` like a caption that stands alone). So the plate and its words are moved, turned and deleted as one, and each line is still typed over. A click on the words goes into them, as on any text in a group; a click on the plate beside the words takes the label.
  - A shape that carries its own words (the `label` of the ready-made designs) was the alternative. It is one element and its fill is changed with the fill tool, but it has one fill and one outline: a ribbon with tails, folds and stitches, a badge with a rim, a tag with a string are drawings. The group was chosen for the craft.
- **An extra may lie under the picture** (`under`), and a card may be less than the box of its frame (`card` in the generator): a print with a burst behind it, leaves that peek out, a record sliding out of its sleeve. In the group such an extra comes before the picture. The magnet's shadow is the group's and follows what is drawn, so a card of any shape casts the right shadow.
- **A caption may be set to a side** (`align: 'left' | 'right'`), the same side in both languages: a magnet is not mirrored for Hebrew, so a lockup of a name, a chip and a small line stands where it was drawn.
- **Words are sized with their plate.** Where the artwork of a magnet is drawn smaller or larger (a magnet made smaller than its artwork, and back), the type of every line beside the picture is sized by the same ratio (`typeSized` in `stage/groups.ts`), not only its box: a caption relied on `autoFit` before, which draws type smaller only once its line is taller than its box, so words on a plate came out too large for it. Made larger, a magnet keeps its artwork and its type, as before.
- **`reframe` carries typed words across kinds.** The lines of a magnet are its captions and the lines of its labels, in the order its group holds them; words that were typed over go to the line of the same place in the next magnet, onto a label or off one.
- **The birthday magnet has its ribbon as a label**, tilted, with a popper firing from behind its end over the corner of the photograph; its card is the sunny ground and its confetti, under the ribbon too. The other nine of the first ten are as they were: none of them draws on its card a plate for words.
  - A birthday magnet that is on a slide already keeps the artwork it has. One that was made after ADR-086 carries its own drawing and is resized as it was. One of the catalogue before ADR-086 is no longer given the catalogue's artwork at its first resize (`freshFrame`): the card of now has no ribbon, and the old caption would stand on bare ground. The rule is general: a frame that has since moved a part of its card onto a label is another design, and a picture without that label beside it is left whole. Clicking the frame again in the panel gives the magnet of now.
- **Ninety more magnets, in nine files of ten** (`scripts/frames-magnets/<file>.mjs`, drawn with `scripts/frames-magnets/kit.mjs`; `scripts/frames-magnets.mjs` is the index). The lead planned every frame before any was drawn (event, composition, window, palette, type, extras, pictures), and one builder drew each file. No two frames of a file share a composition: banners across a corner, type down a side, badges and lower-thirds over the photograph's edge, one heavy cluster against a lockup of type, bunting and garlands along an edge, prints smaller than the box with things behind them, and windows that are round, arched, tilted, torn, notched or the outline of a house.
- **The magnets are shown by set**: family celebrations, birthdays, children, school and army, work, leisure and trips, holidays, styles (`MAGNET_SETS`; a frame of the catalogue says its `set`). The first rows of the group in the overview show the first magnet of every set, then the second of each. "Show all" shows every set under its name, each drawn when it comes near the visible part of the panel, with a row of buttons that shows one set alone. The search of the frames finds a magnet by its name and by its tags in both languages, as it did.
  - The holidays are in: the first ten had left them out as not generic. Ten Jewish holidays (Rosh Hashanah, Sukkot, Hanukkah, Tu BiShvat, Purim, Pesach, Independence Day, Lag BaOmer, Shavuot, Mimouna) and four others (New Year's Eve, a day of love, Christmas, Eid al-Fitr). This was the lead's choice, not the user's word.
  - Sixteen magnets are styles with no event (minimal, black and gold, film, ticket, magazine cover, kraft, scrapbook, boarding pass and others).
  - Every magnet is 7 by 5, lying or upright, as the first ten were.
- **Generated pictures are stickers and textures, carried in the markup.** A cut-out ornament (watercolour flowers, the symbols of a holiday, the characters of a children's theme) is a sticker whose SVG holds the picture as WebP data (`kit.picture`), and a texture (kraft paper, cork, foil) is a pattern of the card that repeats (`kit.tile`), so a larger card has more of it and none is stretched. The renderer's rules for pictures already allow raster data inside an SVG. One call to the image tool gives four cut-outs (a sheet of two by two, each cut to its own box). A numeral, a word or a flag is never generated: an age is a caption, so it is changed.
  - The alternative, the pictures as assets of the deck (as a ready-made design imports its picture), would keep a deck smaller, but a group may then hold several images (`framedPicture` takes the one image of the group for the photograph), adding a frame would wait for an import, and the card's own textures could not use it at all. It stays open.
- **The catalogue and the pictures are in the media library**, by the user's decision of the same night: `Slidr-media/elements/catalogs/frames-catalog.json` is what the generator writes and the app reads, and the generated pictures are sources in `Slidr-media/elements/source/frames/pictures/`. The generator and the files of magnets are scripts of the app and stay in `apps/desktop/scripts`.
- `generate-frames-catalog.mjs` takes `--magnets a,b` (only those files of magnets) and `--out file` (the catalogue is written there, and the app's is left alone), so a file is looked at while it is drawn.

## Also changed on the way

- **Ctrl+A in a text selects the text, not the paragraphs.** The editor's own "select all" took the paragraph nodes too, so what was typed over a selected caption came out bare: no face, size, colour or alignment. This held for every text of the app, not only captions. `Mod-a` in `TextEditor.tsx` now selects from the start of the first line to the end of the last, as "Select all the text" of the menu already did.
- **A line with no Hebrew letter reads from the left in a Hebrew deck** (an age, "00:00", "23A"): `frameExtras` sets a caption's direction by its words. The design check (L15) reported such a line otherwise.
- **The magnets list opens at its top**: the panel was left scrolled to the group it was opened from, and the first set, drawn only once it is in view, stayed empty above the fold.
- `generate-frames-catalog.mjs` looked for a number that did not come out ("NaN") in a frame's whole text, and found it by chance in the data of a picture; it now leaves picture data out of that check.

## Verification

- **Every magnet rendered and looked at, in Hebrew and in English**: on a slide of the app as it lands around a photograph, made wider, taller, larger and half as large, and at thumbnail size as the panel draws it in the light and the dark interface. Each builder did this for its ten; the lead looked at every group's sheets in both languages, then at all hundred over one portrait photograph (a head high in the frame), and at the hundred thumbnails in both themes. Three magnets had a piece on the forehead there and were changed (the glasses of the toast, the thatch of the sukkah, the wreath of Shavuot, now on a corner).
- **Measured, 200 renders of the app's catalogue** (`test-results/frames/tools/render.mjs --app`): the design check reports L11 only (colours and fonts that are not the template's), and L07 for an upright magnet alone on its slide; 608 lines of words, the widest at 85% of its box, none drawn smaller than its size, the smallest type 25px on the slide; no extra stretched or pushed out of the magnet by a resize. On 34 magnets the boxes of the extras overlap more than 16% of the photograph's box: these were looked at one by one, and are pieces on corners and edges whose boxes are mostly empty.
- **Unit tests**: `frames.test.ts` (the hundred in their sets, labels as groups with their words on the plate, extras under the picture, a side that is the same in both languages, words without Hebrew from the left, typed words carried onto a label and off it, an earlier birthday magnet left whole) and `space.test.ts` (words sized with the artwork; a label moved as one and sized with its words) pass, 73 tests. Of the areas touched (elements, stage, text, model, renderer) 781 tests pass. Two failures there are not of this round: `text/toolbar/shared.test.ts` does not load (`Slidr-media/.../shapes.json?raw` is refused under the test runner) and `shell/designRules.test.ts` names `designKit.ts`, which left the repository the same night.
- **E2E on a server of its own**: `elements-panel.spec.ts`, 17 of 17 (the sets and their buttons; a label taken by its plate, dragged as one, typed on by a click and keeping its type; a label that keeps its size when the magnet is made wider). `text-format.spec.ts` 31 of 32 and `table-editing.spec.ts` 11 of 12, for the change to Ctrl+A: the one failure in each waits for a button of row A that another session's work on the top tools removed that night. No other E2E file was run.
- **Export**: six magnets (a round window, a label, an upright one with generated pictures, a card of a texture, a piece under the picture, theme colours), with one made wider and one half as large, were exported to one HTML file (1.9 MB) and the file opened with the network off: it asks for nothing outside itself, reports no error, and draws the magnets as the slide does.
- **Magnets that were on slides already**: the two workspaces open that night held no frame. The magnets of the catalogue of before this round were put through today's code and resized four ways: drawn and resized as they were.

## Open for the user

- **The design of the hundred** has not been judged by the user. The sheets are in `apps/desktop/test-results/frames/work/all/` (`set-<set>-he.png`, `set-<set>-en-p5.png`, `thumbs-<lang>-<theme>.png`).
- **Weight.** The catalogue went from 0.8 MB to 10.2 MB: 8.2 MB of it is 110 generated pictures as data. It is read when the frames are first shown. A magnet with three pictures puts 200 to 250 kB of markup into the deck it is added to. If that is too much: pictures as deck assets (see above), smaller pictures, or fewer of them.
- **The holidays**: whether fourteen is the wish, and whether the four that are not Jewish holidays (New Year's Eve, a day of love, Christmas, Eid al-Fitr) belong.
- **A garland or bunting keeps its size**, as every sticker does: on a much wider magnet it stays at the middle, or its two halves part to the corners (Christmas, family day, camping). A sticker that repeats along an edge would need a new kind of extra.
- **Sample words**: names, places and wording are placeholders; several are gendered (מצטיין, מתגייס, ומהממת, רוני חוגגת) and are typed over. The Hebrew of a few was the builders' doubt: "מסיבת הלידה של יעל", "הכלה בדרך!", "מגנט ליום הולדת שנה", "עיד מבארכ", "דינו־מסיבה".
- **Left-set Hebrew** in a few lockups (first birthday, thirty, sixty) is an editorial choice of the plan, not the natural default.
- **The generated pictures and the catalogue are outside git** (`../Slidr-media`), so this round's content has no history. Thirty pictures that no magnet uses are in `elements/source/frames/pictures/spare/`. 43 calls to the image tool were made, of a cap of 60 the lead set itself.
- **The first screen's search** shows up to nine frames: with a hundred magnets named "מגנט ל…", a query such as "לב" also finds "לבת מצווה" by its beginning, as the search always matched the beginning of a word.
